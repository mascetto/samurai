宮本武蔵 Miyamoto Musashi Harima Province · 60 Duels Undefeated
Niten Ichi-ryū Gorin no Sho Yoshikawa Novel Vagabond Manga

Japan's most celebrated swordsman the two-sword master who never lost a duel, wrote The Book of Five Rings, and inspired centuries of storytelling. His journey from violent youth to philosophical warrior remains one of history's great transformations.

伊藤一刀斎 Itō Ittōsai Izu Province · Founder of Ittō-ryū
Ittō-ryū Single Sword Mystery

A near-mythical figure who founded the most influential school of Japanese swordsmanship — Ittō-ryū. Said to have won over 33 duels. His origins are obscure, his disappearance unexplained. He trained masters who would shape the sword arts for generations.

卜
1489 - 1571 Coming Soon
塚原卜伝 Tsukahara Bokuden Hitachi Province · 19 Duels, 37 Battles
Kashima Shintō-ryū Mutekatsu Three Shoguns

Survived 19 individual duels, 37 battles, and was wounded only six times — always by arrows, never by a sword. He tutored three shoguns and famously taught a vain student "the ultimate secret of the sword" by crossing a river without drawing his blade.

神
1508 - 1577 Coming Soon
上泉信綱 Kamiizumi Nobutsuna Kōzuke Province · Father of Shinkage-ryū
Shinkage-ryū Fukuro Shinai Hosokawa Court

Called "the sword saint" by his contemporaries. Founder of Shinkage-ryū — one of the three great foundations of Japanese sword arts. He developed the fukuro shinai (practice sword) for safer sparring and mastered the concept of mushin, swordless swordsmanship.

柳
1527 — 1606 Available
柳生宗厳 Yagyū School Yamato Province · Shogunal Instructor · Most Famous School
Yagyū Shinkage-ryū Tokugawa No-Sword

The sword school that rose from obscurity to become the official art of the Tokugawa shogunate. Across generations, the Yagyū refined Shinkage-ryū into a philosophy of control, restraint, and the life-giving sword where mastery meant winning without killing.

四十七士 The 47 Rōnin Edo Period · The Akō Incident
Bushidō Giri & Chūgi Chūshingura

Not one man, but forty-seven — masterless samurai who waited nearly two years to avenge the death of their lord Asano Naganori. Their story is Japan's defining tale of loyalty, sacrifice, and the bushidō code. Immortalised in Kabuki, novels, films, and manga worldwide.
